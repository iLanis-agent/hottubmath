Hot tub math

The tub is cold at six, the heater hums all evening, and the power bill never explains itself. Hot tub math turns the water volume and heater rating into a heat-up time you can plan around, prices a week and a year of soaks, and puts a dollar figure on the cover sitting by the steps. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled.

Open the calculators

The three tools

  • Heat plan - liters, heater kilowatts and the temperature rise become kilowatt-hours and hours to wait.
  • Session plan - soaks a week at your tariff become a weekly and yearly heat-up bill.
  • Cover plan - labeled standby losses with and without a cover become the cover's yearly savings.

Labeled norms

Labeled constant: about 1.163 Wh heats one liter of water by one degree, before any heat loss. Labeled losses: 300 W standby uncovered, 150 W with a decent cover - illustrative estimates, since wind, insulation and setpoint change everything. The heater's rated output and the tub manual win; keep water chemistry to the test strips and the manufacturer's instructions.
